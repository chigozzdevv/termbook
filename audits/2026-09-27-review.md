# Termbook implementation and security review

Date: 27 September 2026. Audited revision: `70e3ea3524038c12afb14e4983c71f8f6b0efbd4` on `main`.

## Verdict

Termbook has substantial protocol, server, SDK, and collateral code. It follows the main architecture in `termbook.md`: House-funded restricted accounts on Monad, source-chain collateral, versioned application integrations, and isolated settlement inboxes. It does **not** yet implement the complete specified lifecycle safely. Deployment alone will not finish it.

The highest-priority problems are stale valuation during withdrawals, LP queue economics, repayment availability, recovery finalization, closure ordering, and external implementation changes that evade the runtime code-hash check. Additional work is required for asynchronous application execution, economic loss accounting, and operational monitoring.

The missing standalone client is excluded from this review. SDK orchestration is included because an integrating application depends on it even without Termbook's own client.

## Security and correctness findings

Severity assumes the affected component is enabled with real funds. A high finding can threaten backing, allocation, or a required exit. A medium finding can block normal operation or violate an economic/security guarantee under the stated conditions. Reproductions use local fixtures, not attacks on deployed contracts.

### A01 — High: withdrawals use cached backing after its current value has changed

**Evidence:** [cash withdrawal](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3195), [collateral withdrawal](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:2791), and [snapshot validation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3488).

`_requireFreshHealth` checks the saved Credit revision, snapshot age, and debt. It does not reread the authoritative application state or collateral oracle. Prices and application equity can change without changing the Credit revision. `healthMaxAge` defaults to one day. A borrower can therefore withdraw using an old high collateral/app valuation even when the configured reader would report insufficient backing now. Post-action valuation protects the application-action path, but withdrawals do not run that check.

**Reproduction:** record cash 400 and source value 600 against debt 400; change the mock authoritative source value to zero without changing the Credit revision; withdraw 300 successfully. A subsequent fresh health read reports 25% backing. This demonstrates the stale-reader boundary; it does not simulate a live oracle feed.

**Fix:** obtain current authoritative health atomically for every asset release and other consequential risk decision. Verify reader, oracle, collateral-verifier, and integration status at that point. Apply the withdrawal to that same valuation and enforce the resulting maintenance requirement. A cached read can remain useful for display.

### A02 — High: cancelling a queued LP withdrawal restores old shares at a changed NAV

**Evidence:** [queue creation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/house/HouseVault.sol:264) and [cancellation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/house/HouseVault.sol:299).

Queueing burns shares and creates a fixed asset claim. Cancellation deletes that fixed claim but restores the original number of shares. If the remaining pool earns income in between, the cancelling LP regains participation in income earned while its claim was outside the share supply. Other LPs are diluted. The LP has an option to take its fixed claim or restore its old shares, depending on subsequent pool performance.

**Reproduction:** two LPs supply 10,000 each. One queues a 10,000 claim. The remaining pool earns 10,000. Cancellation restores 10,000 old shares and gives the cancelling LP a 15,000 share claim; the continuously invested LP loses 5,000 of the income allocated to it before cancellation.

**Fix:** choose one explicit queue model. Either queued shares remain exposed to NAV until redemption, or a fixed claim is converted back to shares at the current conservative NAV. Include loss, zero-share-supply, cancellation, and claim-order cases.

### A03 — High: LP queues can make revolving repayments revert; recovery repayments also reserve funds again

**Evidence:** [direct/account repayment](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3137), [inbox repayment](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3171), and [commitment restoration](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/house/HouseVault.sol:405).

Every principal repayment on a revolving Credit calls `restoreCommitment`. That function requires unqueued available cash. A large LP withdrawal queue can consume the incoming cash for this calculation, causing restoration to revert and rolling back the repayment itself. This affects ordinary repayment and Aurora receipts applied through the same accounting path.

Restoration is also unconditional with respect to maturity and recovery state. Repayment during recovery reserves principal again after liquidation released the commitment. Funds are reserved for a facility that cannot draw them.

**Reproductions:** a 9,700 queued claim against a 10,000-funded House causes a 110 repayment, comprising 10 fees and 100 principal, to revert. Separately, fully repaying a liquidating revolving Credit recreates a 400 commitment while its state remains `RecoveryPending`.

**Fix:** make accepted repayment independent of acquiring fresh commitment. Encode how the existing revolving commitment is preserved across repayments and LP queues. End it at maturity/full termination, and implement the spec's distinct partial-recovery suspension rule. Reconcile all repayment paths with that policy.

### A04 — High: full recovery cannot be finalized through the shipped HouseVault

**Evidence:** [finalization and liquidation completion](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3461).

`finalizeRecovery` accepts only the Credit's HouseVault as caller. The shipped HouseVault has no entry point that calls it. `finishLiquidation` requires the flag set by that function. A keeper, borrower, or governance call directly to Core cannot complete this transition for that vault, even after all debt has been paid.

There is also no completed per-Credit write-off/realized-loss and subsequent-recovery allocation lifecycle. House impairment is an increment-only global adjustment, and `fundedReserves` has no funding/accounting implementation. These are necessary when full termination leaves unpaid claims.

**Evidence level:** complete caller-path inspection plus a reproduction showing rejection of direct finalization after full recovery repayment. Impersonating the vault in a unit test would not provide a real entry point.

**Fix:** implement an authorized, reachable House/Core finalization path that verifies remaining assets, application obligations, remote operations, claims, and commitments. Record realized losses and later recoveries per Credit with the required LP and first-loss claim rights. A callable flag setter alone is insufficient.

### A05 — High, conditional on admitting an upgradeable target: proxy upgrades evade runtime pinning

**Evidence:** [call admission](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/integration/IntegrationRegistry.sol:92) and [execution validation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/integration/IntegrationRegistry.sol:206).

The registry compares the target's runtime code hash. A proxy keeps that hash when its implementation changes. Server source checks can detect changes when serving configuration, but already configured SDKs and direct Core calls are not forced through that server check. An app upgrade authority can therefore change the code handling an admitted call without invalidating the onchain policy. Similar scrutiny is needed for upgradeable readers and validators.

**Reproduction:** admit a proxy and `StaticCallValidator`, upgrade the proxy so the same selector executes different code, then call `validateCall`. It still returns true. The attacker in this scenario controls the external upgrade authority; it is not an arbitrary-user upgrade exploit.

**Fix:** admit immutable targets, or require a reviewed guard capable of validating the actual implementation and relevant permissions/configuration onchain. Arbitrary external storage slots cannot simply be read by Core. If an upgrade mechanism cannot be reliably guarded, it must remain outside the admitted custody boundary. Test freezing and the surviving safe recovery route.

### A06 — Medium: a health snapshot can become unusable after ordinary interest accrual

**Evidence:** [draw accrual/check order](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3073) and [exact debt comparison](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3499).

`recordHealth` and a subsequent draw/withdrawal/liquidation commonly occur in separate transactions. The later transaction accrues interest, then requires the old snapshot debt to exactly equal the newly accrued debt. Once even one base unit accrues, an otherwise recent snapshot fails. With realistic token precision, accrual can change debt very frequently. A same-block or atomic batch can avoid this, but normal SDK sequencing does not guarantee one.

**Fix:** calculate current debt and current health within the consequential transaction. Preserve remainder accounting; do not fix this by dropping accrued interest or accepting an old debt denominator.

### A07 — Medium: recovery pays protocol fees before lender principal is restored

**Evidence:** [recovery allocation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3408) and [immediate protocol fee transfer](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/house/HouseVault.sol:374). The required priority is specified in [the loss waterfall](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/termbook.md:575).

Core allocates recovery to fees and interest before principal. HouseVault immediately transfers the protocol percentage of those amounts. Thus an underwater recovery can send value to the protocol while lender principal remains unpaid, contrary to the specification. This matters when a nonzero protocol fee is configured.

**Reproduction:** recover 10 against 410 debt, with a 10-unit accrued fee and a 10% protocol fee. The protocol receives 1 while all 400 of principal remains unpaid.

**Fix:** distinguish ordinary revenue realization from insolvency allocation. Accrue or escrow subordinate protocol entitlements until the lender-principal waterfall permits payment. Apply one tested allocation policy to cash, application, local collateral, and remote receipts.

### A08 — Medium: LP share conversion can use unaccrued loan value

**Evidence:** [deposit conversion](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/house/HouseVault.sol:216), [stored NAV calculation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/house/HouseVault.sol:492), and [permissionless accrual](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:1965).

House share price uses stored `accruedReceivables`. Interest that has accrued with time is only added when Core accrues the Credit. Deposit and withdrawal conversion neither refresh it nor require an up-to-date valuation checkpoint. A new LP can enter at the old price immediately before an accrual and participate in income earned before entering. Known impairment and eventual write-off recovery also need consistent treatment.

**Evidence level:** static accounting trace; no additional reproduction is claimed for this finding.

**Fix:** provide bounded aggregate accrual and conservative loss accounting, or require an enforceable fresh House valuation checkpoint before conversion. The design must remain executable with the maximum supported number of Credits; an unbounded loop is unsuitable.

### A09 — High functional blocker: closure cannot begin with debt and unwind it as specified

**Evidence:** [Core closing entry](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/credit/TermbookCore.sol:3234), [SDK close](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/packages/sdk/src/credit/credit.ts:156), and [specified closure](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/termbook.md:481).

Core requires zero debt before entering `Closing`. The SDK requires enough free account tokens to repay first and does not orchestrate the application's unwind. A Credit whose backing is inside an app cannot use `close()` to freeze, cancel, close, collect proceeds, and repay. A borrower may manually use the separately authorized `exitApp` path while Ready, but that is not the specified closure operation.

The SDK also returns `closed: false` after authorizing a remote return, instead of completing/resuming the full confirmed return lifecycle. Debt-free position ownership release is not implemented by the immutable-Core CreditAccount.

**Reproduction:** a correctly prepared, funded Credit with debt cannot call `startClosing`.

**Fix:** permit an authorized transition to a frozen `Closing` state with outstanding debt. Persist the selected unwind/retention plan and reconcile bounded exit steps, cash, liabilities, repayment, approvals, surplus, source return, and completion. Make retries resume that state. Test an account with zero initial free cash.

### A10 — Medium: anyone can invalidate another wallet's API login challenge

**Evidence:** [unauthenticated nonce endpoint](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/server/src/app.ts:27), [nonce mutation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/server/src/auth/auth.repo.ts:4), and [authentication](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/server/src/auth/auth.service.ts:44).

Requesting a nonce increments the single current nonce for the supplied wallet. An unauthenticated caller can repeatedly request nonces for a victim, invalidating signatures between wallet approval and API submission. No application rate limiter is configured. This is an API availability attack, not theft of an onchain signature.

**Fix:** issue independent expiring random challenges and consume the exact challenge atomically. Bound outstanding challenges and apply rate limits. Separately, sensitive admin/application writes should use authorizations bound to their intended action and payload; the current message is a generic login statement.

### A11 — Medium functional gap: a funded LP inbox cannot be cancelled before expiry

**Evidence:** [SettlementInbox cancellation/refund](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/src/monad/routing/SettlementInbox.sol:100) and [LP specification](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/termbook.md:821).

`cancel()` requires `available() == 0`. Once an LP's partial or full receipt is accounted in the inbox, cancellation reverts and refund remains unavailable until the deadline. The specified authorized cancellation before minting is therefore missing precisely when money is waiting there. There is also no authorized revision path for deposit bounds, minimum shares, and deadline.

**Fix:** implement a revisioned LP authorization with an atomic race among revision, cancellation, mint, and refund. Cancellation must handle existing receipts and force subsequent receipts into the fixed refund path without allowing another source funding attempt.

### A12 — Medium reliability defect: normal queue job IDs are rejected by BullMQ

**Evidence:** [job ID creation](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/server/src/queue/queue.client.ts:39).

The generated ID is `kind:identifier`, for example `credit.tracked:0xabc`. The installed BullMQ rejects that format with `Custom Id cannot contain :`. Its validator was exercised directly during this review and returned that error. Queue submission catches it and uses the MongoDB outbox, so this does not mean every job is lost. It means Redis delivery does not work as configured for those IDs, and fallback retry behavior replaces the intended queue behavior.

**Fix:** use a deterministic encoded/hash ID without a colon, test it against the real BullMQ API, and test both Redis and Mongo delivery with restart/retry/idempotency cases. See [BullMQ job ID rules](https://docs.bullmq.io/guide/jobs/job-ids).

### Specific integration-platform work still required

Core should stay venue agnostic. The platform must make integration admission repeatable, while each plugin supplies authoritative app positions, liabilities, pricing, calls, and borrower-independent exit behavior. A plugin can be contributed by an integrating app; Core does not need a hardcoded perps venue.

The new [evidence validator](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/server/src/integration/integration.builder.ts:35) does useful checks: canonical finalized successful receipts, a Core call for each declared capability, bundle/target/selector matching, positive live application value, empty recovered position/pending counts, recovery events, and subsequent account-funded repayment. However, it does not itself prove the full [16-part acceptance gate](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/termbook.md:1099).

Required additions include structured before/after economic and ownership assertions; actual placement/reduction/cancellation/closure results; non-cooperative recovery; stale-price, permission-loss, proxy-upgrade and paused-app behavior; and maximum-size/partial-fill/interrupted-recovery cases. An opaque `evidenceHash` and successful transactions need an independently reviewable report behind them. Current publication remains dependent on manual review for those claims.

`AppHealthReader` caps observed positions and pending entries at 32 each. It does not reserve the worst-case slots an asynchronous action can create. The Core does not yet provide the specified durable pending-action lifecycle for partial fills, cancellation races, timeout, refunds, and later exposure reconciliation. The SDK currently labels an executed action pending from its manifest settlement flag.

`FlowExecutor` and `AppGateway` exist, but are not wired into Core as the specified bounded multi-step execution framework. Their existence should not be counted as completed dependency/strategy support. The SDK's `slippageBound` helper is not used by the action builder to freeze an authoritative reference-price bound; that guarantee must be explicit in the admitted capability and runtime validator.

## What the tests prove, and what they miss

The suite contains useful accounting, signature, proof, custody, refund, application-cash, and fault-case checks. It also contains a fuzz test for using account cash before borrowing. Nevertheless, file names and aggregate passing counts overstate lifecycle coverage:

- [E2E liquidation test](/Users/chigozzdev/Desktop/Termbook/contracts/evm/test/e2e/liquidation-flow.t.sol:12) only expects rejection of an invalid acceptance; it does not liquidate anything.

- There is no broad stateful accounting-invariant suite or checked-in CI workflow enforcing the entire EVM/TypeScript/Solana matrix. The root `scripts/test.sh` does not run the Solana tests.

## Work required before deployment is the main remaining step

1. **Correct asset-release and House accounting rules.** Fix A01–A08 together with a written cash/commitment/share/loss invariant model. Test cross-effects: queued withdrawals, partial repayments, insolvency, maturity, cure, and late receipts.
2. **Complete the lifecycle.** Implement indebted closure, reachable full recovery, write-off/later-recovery allocation, first-loss/reserve accounting, and the spec's retained partial-recovery commitment. Test borrower-independent exit and account ownership/permission cleanup.
3. **Complete integration execution and evidence.** Finish bounded multi-step and asynchronous state tracking, slot reservations, session/price bounds, runtime dependency guards, and the full acceptance harness. Keep integration-specific semantics in plugins.
4. **Finish omitted collateral and LP operations.** Add verified collateral top-ups and remote partial withdrawals if retaining the full current spec. Add revisioned LP cancellation/bounds and refunds. Every enabled operation needs an actual retry/reconciliation path.
5. **Build the operating services and enforce tests.** Add the continuous risk/default/recovery monitor, durable event/index reconciliation, queue/auth fixes, metrics/alerts, restart safety, and CI across all three languages. Replace misleading lifecycle tests with complete ones and add stateful conservation tests.

After those code tasks, the deployment/admission phase still includes exact contracts/programs, multisig and timelock roles, Guardian/emitter bindings, token/feed and risk configuration, funded Houses, keepers, supported Aurora routes, secrets/infrastructure, reproducible artifacts, and measured verification/execution budgets. Run live normal-exit and forced-recovery exercises for each enabled combination, including source return, partial/late delivery, failed LP mint/refund, interruption, and resumption. Independent security review should examine the fixes and the exact deployed integration/proof boundaries before real funds.

## Review limits

This is a source and local-execution audit, not formal verification or an independent cryptographic certification. The findings are substantiated where indicated; absence of a finding is not proof that the remaining code or dependencies have no defects. No runtime code, specification, deployment, keys, or remote state was modified. The only additions are this report and the local audit reproductions.
